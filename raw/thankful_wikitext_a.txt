'''Thankful Villages''' (also known as '''Blessed Villages'''; {{Langx|cy|Pentrefi Diolchgar}})<ref>{{cite news|work=BBC News (in Welsh)|url=https://www.bbc.com/cymrufyw/23472102|title=Taith i gofio 'pentrefi diolchgar' (''Tour in remembrance of "thankful villages"'')|accessdate=8 November 2018}}</ref><ref>[https://www.mearns.org/stcyrus/pdf/stcyrus1007.pdf St Cyrus – an example of the use of "Blessed Villages" in section 'St Cyrus War Memorial' of the St Cyrus Newsletter, October/ November 2007, at mearns.org/stcyrus] Accessed 11 November 2017</ref> are settlements in England and Wales from which all their members of the armed forces survived [[World War I]]. The term Thankful Village was popularised by the writer [[Arthur Mee]] in the 1930s; in ''Enchanted Land'' (1936), the introductory volume to ''[[The King's England]]'' series of guides, he wrote that a Thankful Village was one which had lost no men in the war because all those who left to serve came home again. His initial list identified 32 villages. There are tens of thousands of villages and towns in the United Kingdom.

In an October 2013 update,<ref name="TV">{{cite web|last=Norman Thorpe, Rod Morris and Tom Morgan|title=The Thankful Villages|url=http://www.hellfirecorner.co.uk/thankful.htm|publisher=Hellfire corner|accessdate=5 November 2013}}</ref> researchers identified 52 [[civil parishes in England]] and Wales from which all serving personnel returned.  There are no Thankful Villages identified in Scotland or Ireland yet (all of Ireland was then part of the [[United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland|United Kingdom]]).<ref name=bbc_thankful/>

Fourteen of the English and Welsh villages are considered "doubly thankful", in that they also lost no service personnel during [[World War II]].<ref name=bbc_thankful>{{cite web | url= https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/magazine-15671943 | title= Thankful villages: The places where everyone came back from the wars | first= Jon | last= Kelly | publisher= BBC News | work= BBC News Magazine | date= 11 November 2011 | accessdate= 12 November 2011}}</ref> These are marked in italics in the list below (note: while the list includes 17 of these, not all have been verified).

==France==

In France, where the human cost of war was higher than in Britain, there were only twelve villages in all of France with no men lost from World War I.<ref>Grégory Bozonnet, La sécurité. La mémoire, Armand Colin, 2016, p. 87.</ref>  One of these, [[Thierville]], also suffered no losses in the [[Franco-Prussian War]] and World War II, France's other bloody wars of the modern era.<ref>Jérôme Duhamel (Paris 1990). ''Grand Inventaire du Génie Français'', p. 196: "Between 1919 and 1925, a war memorial was erected in every community in France, with one single exception: the village of Thierville in the department of the Eure, the only French village which had no dead to mourn, not in 1870, nor in 14–18, nor in 39–45" Also quoted in Thorpe, Morris and Morgan's "The Thankful Villages" referenced above.</ref><ref>{{cite news|work=BBC News|author=Kelly, Jon|date=11 November 2011|title=Thankful villages: The places where everyone came back from the wars|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/magazine-15671943|accessdate=9 November 2018}}</ref>

== In popular culture ==

Between 2016 and 2018, singer-songwriter [[Darren Hayman]] released a trilogy of albums inspired by and written in-situ at the Thankful Villages. 54 villages were covered, including [[Welbury]], North Yorkshire, not in the 52 listed above.<ref>{{cite news|title=We Lucky Few|url=https://inews.co.uk/explainers/iq/darren-hayman-thankful-villages-album-first-world-war/|accessdate=16 July 2016|publisher=i (newspaper)|date=31 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last1=Petridis|first1=Alexis|title=Darren Hayman: No sleep till Upper Slaughter|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2016/jul/12/darren-hayman-thankful-villages-music-first-world-war-hefner|accessdate=16 July 2016|work=The Guardian|date=12 July 2016}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Thankful Villages – A project about rural life by Darren Hayman |url=https://thankful-villages.co.uk/ |access-date=2026-06-09 |website=[[Darren_Hayman#Thankful_Villages_and_The_Hayman_Kupa_Band|thankful-villages.co.uk]]}}</ref>
